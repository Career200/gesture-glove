Thumb Ring

A D-pad you wear.

A ring on the index finger. Swipe your thumb across it in one of four directions, or tap it. That's the whole vocabulary. It pairs as an ordinary Bluetooth keyboard, so anything that understands arrow keys already understands the ring, with no app to install.

The pitch, three ways

What it does

A tiny wearable directional controller: a ring worn on the finger that lets you issue discreet, eyes-free commands by moving your thumb. It's always available and needs no touchpad or screen. Essentially, a D-pad you wear.

What it explores

A persistent, body-worn input method built around thumb-to-finger interaction: tactile, eyes-free control, gesture recognition, keeping accidental input out, and the smallest command vocabulary that still works across contexts and devices.

How it works

An MPU-6050 motion sensor picks up the friction and micro-vibration of the thumb moving across the ring. A copper-tape contact pad gates it, so motion only counts while the thumb is on the ring. An ESP32-C3 classifies the gesture and sends a keypress over Bluetooth, for well under $15 in parts.

Where it's at

Nothing is built yet. Before any electronics, the ring has to be something you'd keep on your finger all day. So the first step is printed dummies at the real size and weight, on bendable wire bands. After that, the open question is whether a cheap motion sensor can tell four thumb swipes apart and ignore everything else a hand does.
